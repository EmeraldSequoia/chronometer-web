Inspector is a live data explorer for the astronomy engine shared by Emerald Chronometer and Emerald Observatory. It displays the engine's values directly — calendar and clock quantities, sidereal and solar time, the equation of time, and positions, distances, and rise/set/transit times for the Sun, the Moon, and the planets — updating live as the clock runs, for any time and location.

Reading the catalog

Values are grouped Time → Sun → Moon → Eclipse → Mercury … Neptune. Angles are in degrees; rise, set, and transit are local times at the configured location (shown as "—" when the event does not occur, for example during polar day or night); distances are given in both astronomical units and kilometers. Each planet shows two distances: geo (from Earth) and helio (from the Sun), qualified the same way as its ecliptic coordinates. Each value is fully re-evaluated on its own schedule (fractional seconds many times per second, coordinates every second, rise/set times once a minute) and animated smoothly in between.

The Eclipse group

An eclipse needs two things at once: a new or full moon, and the Moon near the plane of Earth's orbit. The Eclipse group shows both conditions and the verdict. Separation is how close the Moon is to new or full — the Moon−Sun difference in ecliptic longitude (0° at new moon, 180° at full) and the true angular elongation. Moon ecl. lat is the Moon's distance from the ecliptic plane; an eclipse requires it to be near zero, which happens when the Moon is near one of its orbit's nodes. Nearest node is the node the Moon is currently closest to — ascending or descending, the crossing the Moon just made or is about to make — with its ecliptic longitude and how far it sits from the point where new or full moons occur. When that gap is small, the Moon reaches new or full just as it crosses the ecliptic there, and an eclipse follows. Level is the eclipse indicator shown on the Basel watch face: the needle value runs from 0 to 3 (below 1 a total eclipse is in progress, 1–2 partial, above 2 none), and the kind text matches Basel's wheel — None, Sun/Moon not up, Partial/Annular/Total Solar, or Partial/Total Lunar.

Precision and accuracy

Chronometer and Observatory never display a value more precisely than the underlying astronomical algorithms can support. The Inspector deliberately breaks that rule: its readouts show more digits than the engine's accuracy warrants. The extra digits are there so you can watch a value move — how fast it is changing, in which direction, and when it reverses — which a data explorer can only show by running past the point where the digits stop being meaningful. Treat the trailing digits as rate information, not as measurement; the engine's real accuracy is described in "Accuracy of the Astronomical Algorithms" under General Help Topics, above.

Time and location

The time controller (under the clock) and the location card work exactly as in Chronometer and Observatory, and all three apps share their settings — set a time or place here and the other apps follow. The Browser time row at the bottom of the Time group shows your device's raw clock for comparison with the Inspector's display time; it is dimmed while the clock is stopped.
