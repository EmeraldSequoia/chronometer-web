Eclipse Table

Every solar and lunar eclipse from 2011 through 2041 — solar and lunar eclipses, listed by the moment of maximum eclipse in UTC, with the same instant on your own clock beside it. The location is where the eclipse is greatest: for a solar eclipse that point sees the deepest coverage of the Sun (for partial eclipses it always falls at sunrise or sunset along the day/night line); for a lunar eclipse it is where the Moon is straight overhead — the whole night side of Earth sees a lunar eclipse. Penumbral lunar eclipses are omitted: the shading is barely perceptible by eye.

The Observatory link opens that instant and place in Observatory, with its eclipse simulator active; the Chronometer link opens the Basel, Venezia, and Selene watch faces there (Venezia showing the Sun for solar eclipses, the Moon for lunar ones). Both arrive with time frozen at maximum, in the eclipse location’s own time zone — press play to watch the eclipse unfold. Details opens the eclipse’s page on EclipseWise.

A hybrid solar eclipse is total along the middle of its path and annular only briefly where the path begins or ends; the point of greatest eclipse lies in the middle, so its links show a total eclipse. Stand near an end of the path instead and the same eclipse appears as a thin ring.
